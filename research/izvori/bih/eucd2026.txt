Strengthening Understanding and Implementation of the Convention on Anti-

Concept Note 
 
 
Background 
 
Bosnia and Herzegovina signed the Convention on 3 December 1997, and ratified it on 8 September 1998, with 
the Convention entering into force for Bosnia and Herzegovina on 1 March 1999. In this initial transparency 
report submitted in February 2000, Bosnia and Herzegovina reported the presence of mine areas under its 
jurisdiction or control. Even prior to the Convention  entering into force, Bosnia and Herzegovina has been 
working to clear its national territory of the threat of anti-personnel mines. In accordance with Article 5 of the 
Convention, Bosnia and Herzegovina undertook to destroy or ensure the destruction of all anti-personnel mines 
in these areas as soon as possible but not later than 1 March 2009. 
 
Despite these efforts, Bosnia and Herzegovina has a signification remaining challenge  to address. Due to  
different reported circumstances, Bosnia and Herzegovina has had to make use of the extension request 
process of the Convention on three separate occasions. The last request for extension of its deadline was 
submitted by Bosnia and Herzegovina in 2020 which was granted by the Eighteenth Meeting of the States 
Parties for six years until 1 March 2027.With the deadline approaching, Bosnia and Herzegovina will be required 
to submit a request for a further extension by 31 March 2026 for consideration by the Twenty -Third Meeting 
of the States Parties later this year.  
 
The national dialogue will provide a platform for Bosnia and Herzegovina to share progress made since the last 
extension request period, raise awareness of the challenges faced and present the new work plan. The National 
Stakeholder Dialogue will also provide space for discussion on the implications of the new national mine action 
law and standards, reflections on the plans for survey, clearance and mine risk education for the extension 
period, status of information management, considerations related to sustaining capacities in mine-free regions, 
and victim assistance provisions. This will support the BHMAC in preparing a comprehensive, accurate, and well-
informed extension request, drawing on the perspectives of all relevant stakeholders including affected 
communities.  
 
The Dialogue is supported by the European Union and with financial support from a global project1 adopted by 
the Council of the European Union in support of the recently adopted Siem Reap-Angkor Action Plan 2025-2029 
and the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention. 
 
Participation and expected outcomes 
 
The EUCD seeks to support an engaged dialogue where Bosnia and Herzegovina can clearly address the above 
mentioned objectives with stakeholders including those representing mine action donors, national and 
international mine clearance organisations, UN agencies, and civil society.  
 
The Dialogue aims to bring together demining programme stakeholders and partners to strengthen 
collaboration, present feedback from national stakeholders on the draft extension request that Bosnia and 
Herzegovina will submit for consideration by the States  Parties and help shape a comprehensive path toward

completing Article 5 implementation. Held over two days and structured around presentations and targeted 
breakout discussions, the Dialogue will provide Bosnia and Herzegovina with an opportunity to reflect on 
recommendations and identify actions that will  support the successful implementation of its mine clearance 
programme. 
 
Expected outcomes and follow ups 
 
 Through the Dialogue, Bosnia and Herzegovina would socialize its draft request for an extended mine 
clearance deadline and receive inputs from all stakeholders, incorporating this information into  its national 
mine plan for implementation of Article 5. 
 
 After the dialogue, Bosnia and Herzegovina would be in a position to produce a detailed outcome document 
highlighting,  
 
a. geographically specific information on remaining areas requiring survey and clearance;  
b. methodologies to address remaining areas; 
c. mine risk education and reduction efforts and challenges; 
d. victim assistance efforts and challenges; 
e. challenges faced in fulfilling its mine clearance obligation and stakeholder’s recommendations to 
address them; 
f. objectives and milestones to address identified challenges; and, 
g. recommendations for further action(s). 
 
 Through this Dialogue and outcome document, Bosnia and Herzegovina would gain additional 
understandings on to strengthen implementation of key aspects of the Convention, particularly on matters 
related to the implementation of Bosnia and Herzegovina’s commitments  under Article 5, and contribute 
to the development of a high quality extension request.
